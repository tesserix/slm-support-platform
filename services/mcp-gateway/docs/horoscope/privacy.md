# Privacy of your birth details

Your birth date, time, and place are sensitive in a way most app data isn't — they're effectively unchangeable about you, and they appear in identity-fraud playbooks. We treat them accordingly.

## Where they're stored

- Birth date, time, and place are stored encrypted at rest, scoped to your user account.
- They're not used for advertising, not shared with third parties, and not included in any analytics export.
- We don't index them on any public profile field — even if you set your profile to "public", strangers cannot see your birth details.

## Who can see them

- **You** — always, on the Birth Details page.
- **Premium astrologers in 1-1 consultation sessions** — only the birth details required for the consultation, only during the session. They don't get persistent access.
- **Support staff helping you debug a chart issue** — only with your explicit consent in the support chat, and only the specific field they need.

## Can I read horoscope without entering birth details?

Yes. Sun-sign daily readings need only the **date** (and a default time of noon, default place of the equator) — that's the free tier and the most cautious. If that's all you want, leave the time and place fields blank in Settings.

The trade-off is that personal transit readings and any deeper chart interpretation **require** an exact time and place; without those, our reading templates fall back to sun-sign-level interpretations.

## Deleting your birth details

From **Settings → Birth details → Clear**. This wipes the encrypted record. Your account stays, but personal transit readings stop until you re-enter details.

If you want to delete everything (account, readings history, etc.), use **Settings → Privacy → Delete account**. There's a 30-day cooling-off; you can cancel any time within that window.

## I'm worried about prediction accuracy implying my birth details were guessed

Predictions are templated from the **chart features** (planet positions, aspects, dashas) — never from your raw birth date / time as identifiers. A prediction can be specific to your chart without leaking the underlying data; the chart is a representation, not the data itself.
